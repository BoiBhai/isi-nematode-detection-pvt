# Nematode anatomy
* Dorsal arch: In nematology, the dorsal arch refers to the upper curved portion of cuticular ridges (striae) located above the anus and tail terminus in the perineal pattern of mature female root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne species).


# Perineal pattern differences
* Convention: Female root-knot nematodes are commonly differentiated at the species level using perineal patterns.
* Location: The posterior (tail) region of mature females, surrounding the vulva and anus.
* Key features: Cuticular ridges (striae), dorsal arch height and shape, lateral lines or fields, tail terminus, and phasmid positions.
* How it is viewed: Dissected from root galls, cleared/mounted on slides or stubs, and examined under light microscopy or scanning electron microscopy (SEM). You can read more about root-knot nematode perineal pattern development and perineal patterns of root-knot nematodes.


## Practical Limitations
• Intraspecific variation: Patterns can vary within a single species due to developmental stage, age, or host effects, so examining multiple females per sample is standard practice.
• Overlapping traits: Some species share similar features (for example, M. enterolobii and M. incognita can look alike).
• Complementary methods: Because morphological overlap and sample preparation artifacts occur, labs often pair perineal pattern analysis with isoenzyme electrophoresis or molecular methods (such as PCR/SCAR markers) for confirmation.
